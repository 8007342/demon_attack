# Gargoyle ground army: research notes

Research for the demon ground army design (`plan/gargoyle-army-style-guide.md`, OpenSpec change `openspec/changes/gargoyle-ground-army/`). Date: 2026-11-13.

**Purpose and limits.** These notes collect **transferable principles**: silhouette language, caste structure, how organic and mechanical technology read, how a unit's role reads, and faction colour coding. Franchise material appears only as evidence for a general principle. No unit, name, character, shape or artwork from any franchise is to be copied or closely imitated. The project's own rule applies: "Never imitate an existing game's creatures" ([style-guide.md](style-guide.md)). Prompts never name a franchise, studio or artist, not even in a negative prompt (the same rule as the music direction).

**Method and source quality.** Web searches ran on 2026-11-13. WebFetch failed with a connection reset on every attempt that day (Wikipedia, Engadget, GameSpot, Stardock, Warhammer Community, gamecloud), so the claims below rest on search-result excerpts, not on full pages. Each claim is tagged:
- **[A]**: an institutional, scholarly or primary developer source.
- **[B]**: a reputable secondary source or an encyclopedia mirror.
- **[C]**: a fan wiki, blog or forum. Use it only as a hint.

Before quoting any line in public-facing text, check it against the full page.

## 1. Gargoyles and grotesques: history and iconography

| Finding | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| *Gargoyle* comes from Old French *gargouille*, "throat" and also "waterspout". The verb *gargouiller* means to gurgle, like liquid in a pipe. The word entered Middle English around 1280. [B] | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gargoyle (read through a mirror excerpt: https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Gargoyle) · https://etymologist.ai/word/gargoyle/article [C] |
| **Strictly, a gargoyle is a working spout.** Rainwater runs along a channel cut into the figure's back and leaves through its mouth, which keeps water off the masonry. Carved figures without that job are *grotesques*, *chimeras* or *bosses*. [B] | https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Gargoyle · https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Chimera |
| Lion-head spouts already appear in Egyptian and Greek architecture. Viollet-le-Duc dated the earliest medieval examples to about 1220 at Laon, with systematic use in Paris by about 1240. London's 1724 Building Act made downpipes compulsory, and gargoyles declined. Art Deco revived them as ornament. [B] | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gargoyle · https://doloresherrero.com/en/the-gargoyles/ [C] |
| The famous Notre-Dame chimeras, including the "Stryge", are **19th-century inventions** from Viollet-le-Duc's restoration (about 1847–1864). They were decorative from the start, imagined as what the medieval builders "might have placed there". [A/B] | https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/notre-dame-portraits-celebrate-restoration-with-vintage-photography · https://www.notredamedeparis.fr/en/?p=3421 · https://mymodernmet.com/notre-dame-cathedral-grotesques/ |
| **The protective (apotropaic) reading**, that grotesques scare off the demons of air and weather, is popular but has almost no medieval documentation. Rival readings call them ornamental *drôleries* or teaching images. [A] | https://journals.openedition.org/cem/19950?lang=en · https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3636650/download · https://countrylife.co.uk/?p=253676 · https://doloresherrero.com/en/symbolism-in-images-of-gargoyles/ [C] |
| Gothic structural vocabulary: **flying buttresses** carry the vault's thrust outside the walls. **Pinnacles** began as weights that pressed that thrust down and later became decoration. **Rose windows** are wheels of radiating stone tracery. **Tracery** is the thin stone frame that holds the glass. [B] | https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1649/ · https://mymodernmet.com/gothic-architecture-characteristics |
| **The masons' lodge** was the workshop and drawing office on the building site. Grades ran apprentice → journeyman → master. A journeyman rose by producing a *masterpiece* that the wardens examined. A new master received a personal **mason's mark**, and disgrace could take it away. [B/C] | https://brewminate.com/the-medieval-guild-apprentice-journeyman-and-master/ · https://www.durhamcityfreemen.org/downloads/masons-history-booklet.pdf · https://historylearning.com/medieval-england/medieval-masons |
| A medieval **siege tower was called a "belfry"**: a wheeled, armoured timber tower built on site that needed level ground. The trebuchet was a counterweight engine. [B] | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_tower · https://www.britannica.com/technology/military-technology/The-castle |

### Principles extracted

1. **The spout is the gargoyle.** The defining trait is functional: a channel runs along the back and out of the mouth. That gives us an original and literal weapon grammar: *every weapon of the army is a carved mouth fed by a visible channel on its back.* A glowing channel filling up toward the mouth is a telegraph that reads at a glance.
2. **Gargoyle, grotesque and chimera are a built hierarchy, not a species.** The army can treat architectural ornament as rank, with no real-world religious symbol needed. Cathedral parts give an original naming source (plinth, corbel, crocket, reliquary, belfry, carillon, buttress, chapterhouse, thurible) that no game franchise owns as a unit roster.
3. **Structure becomes anatomy.** Flying buttresses are already arched legs; pinnacles are already horns; rose windows are already rotating launchers. Each Gothic element suggests a machine part, so the army is "a cathedral that walks to war" and is not borrowed from any faction.
4. **The masons' lodge is a caste system with a visible sign of rank.** The grades apprentice, journeyman and master show their rank through a mark and a masterpiece. Our castes do the same: rank shows in carved ornament and gilding, never in text.
5. **The 19th-century chimeras show that inventing ornament in an old style is legitimate.** Our units can be new while staying true to Gothic vocabulary.
6. **Avoid overt religious symbols.** Grotesques were never symbols themselves. The design uses architecture (arches, spires, tracery, bells), not crosses or saints. A cross silhouette is already taken by the allies' cyan distress cross.

## 2. Alien and demonic armies: transferable patterns

| Pattern | Evidence | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Biological swarm with castes.** Tiny expendable bodies, mid-sized warriors and rare large leader-forms that relay command. If the leaders die, the swarm loses coordination. Weapons are part of the anatomy and ammunition is "grown". [C, official overview A] | Warhammer Community and fan or rules summaries | https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/AaqhM4th/the-hive-mind-speaks-how-the-warhammer-studio-updated-classic-tyranids/ · https://goonhammer.com/the-lore-explainer-codex-tyranids · https://warhammer40000.com/games/faction/tyranids/ |
| **Literary hive castes.** Warriors and workers are directed by a brain caste that is never seen; the queen only reproduces. [C] | Heinlein's *Starship Troopers* (1959), via commentary | https://matthewwquin.substack.com/p/enders-game-hive-civilizations-and · https://allthetropes.org/wiki/Starship_Troopers_(novel) |
| **Demon bound into a machine.** A "daemon engine" is a self-aware fusion of daemon and war-metal; no two are alike, and the machine slowly overtakes the daemon's identity. It fills the tank role in its army. [C] | Lexicanum and Warhammer Community | https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Daemon_Engines · https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Soul_Grinders · https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/w1Z9RgsQ/this-coven-of-converted-soul-grinders-has-a-daemon-engine-for-every-occasion/ |
| **Biomechanics.** Anatomy and machinery merge until a thing belongs to neither nature nor industry; the effect is unsettling. [B/C] | H. R. Giger profiles | https://ocula.com/artists/hr-giger/ · https://courses.bowdoin.edu/italian-2500-spring-2020-knlamm/world-science-fiction/h-r-giger-a-biomechanical-mind/ |
| **Hell as a working machine.** Ordinary objects (instruments) are scaled up and turned into engines of torment, and hybrids are made from mismatched parts. [A/B] | Bosch, the right panel of *The Garden of Earthly Delights* | https://earlymusicmuse.com/bosch2/ · https://smarthistory.org/?p=1592 |
| **Walking architecture.** A building on legs reads at once as "fortress plus creature". Its charm comes from piled-up parts (chimneys, turrets, pipes) on spindly legs. [C] | Howl's Moving Castle commentary | https://www.sfcrowsnest.info/steampunk-field-guide/entries/howls-moving-castle.html · https://deepfocusreview.com/reviews/howls-moving-castle |
| **Gothic horror colour.** Hammer's look was dark stone and heavy drapes against **saturated red accents** in Technicolor, a grand set played against a lurid detail. [C] | Hammer Films commentary | https://www.horrifiedmagazine.co.uk/film/the-uniqueness-of-the-hammer-camera/ · https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3760143/how-hammers-horror-of-dracula-redefined-vampires/ |
| **Silhouette before dogma.** The 2016 Doom team was told to "forget Doom dogma" and design unique, well-defined silhouettes the audience associates with a character at once. A later game made colour coding a systematic signal. [B] | Shacknews, Bethesda, Game Developer | https://www.shacknews.com/article/99662/stairway-to-badass-the-making-and-remaking-of-doom?page=3 · https://bethesda.net/en-US/news/the-art-of-doom-from-mars-to-hell · https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-doom-eternal-owns-the-quot-contrivances-quot-of-video-games |
| **Three factions, three material languages.** An RTS with three distinct races kept 12–14 units each and gave each race its own aesthetic and silhouettes, designed for the **top-down three-quarter view**. Saturated team colour was later toned down so it still read without looking garish. [A/B] | Blizzard art direction (Didier, Browder) | https://www.gamespot.com/articles/blizzard-talks-starcraft-ii-art-design/1100-6171176/ · https://www.engadget.com/2011/10/25/how-blizzard-makes-the-starcraft-2-races-so-iconic/ · https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/starcraft/20719767 · https://www.gosugamers.net/news/7571-graphic-change-in-the-works-for-starcraft-ii |

### Principles extracted

7. **Three tech languages: grown, built and carved.** Swarm factions read as *grown* (soft curves, asymmetry, chitin). Human and industrial factions read as *built* (welds, rivets, panels and right angles: our allies' grey-blue plate). Psionic and zealot factions read as *crafted* (smooth plates, gold trim, glowing crystal). Our army takes a fourth position no one owns: **carved**. Its forms are dressed stone with chisel facets, cast-iron bands, lead flashing and chains, and the stone itself is alive: it blinks, snarls and cracks. This keeps it a clear third party to the grey-blue welded allies and the flying, membrane-winged swarm, while sharing the swarm's faces, horns and wings.
8. **Machine and creature fusion, made friendly.** The daemon-engine idea (a creature bound into a machine) is a general archetype, used as a principle only: every vehicle has a **face** and at least one living part (blinking eyes, a breathing wing-mantlet, a twitching tail). The game's tone forbids gore, so the binding is stone-and-iron masonry, never flesh and machinery.
9. **Caste through scale and relay.** Swarm armies show caste through size and leader-relays. We keep the general rule (many small, fewer medium, one rare tall leader whose loss scatters the small) and express it through architecture: rubble → dressed stone → tracery → spire.
10. **Hell scales up ordinary things.** Bosch's giant instruments suggest the army's support units: a bell that rallies, a censer that lays smoke, a mason's hod that repairs. Familiar objects at monstrous scale read instantly at small size because their outline is already known.
11. **Dark stone, red accents.** Hammer's palette matches the game's existing rule: dark, low-chroma hulls with saturated warm lights kept for eyes, weapons and telegraphs.
12. **Silhouette before lore.** Design each unit's black-fill shape first, against the three-quarter camera; materials and colour come after.

## 3. Readability at small sprite sizes

| Finding | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| When a battle fills the screen, readability depends on **strong silhouettes plus consistent materials**. Shape, material **and motion** together make a faction feel different. [B, developer journal] | https://www.stardock.com/games/article/539126/ashes-ii-dev-journal-8-the-actors-of-war-on-stage-pt-1 · https://www.stardock.com/games/article/487404/dev-journal-designing-alien-units |
| Telling **unit types apart inside one faction** is harder than telling factions apart. Give each unit a clear role and avoid redundant roles. [C] | https://waywardstrategy.com/2018/05/17/unit-design-clarity-of-roles-and-redundancy/ · https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/Stormgate/comments/18d8ge2/as_a_viewer_i_cant_tell_whats_going_on |
| A counter-example: units "lack strong silhouettes, and their size, color schemes, and animations are too similar". Sharp-edged against rounded-edge vehicles was the one shape language players could read. [C] | https://steamcommunity.com/app/1486920/discussions/0/594013058474466051 |
| The production test: a design must read **in greyscale, at a distance and in motion**. Thumbnail it as a solid black shape at about 32 × 32. Order the work silhouette → colour → detail. [C] | https://rocketbrush.com/blog/shape-language-in-game-character-design-how-to-make-characters-readable-and-consistent · https://medium.com/@xavierck/character-readability-in-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-68c41d454465 |
| For pixel art, **value contrast beats hue**. Check in greyscale, keep a sprite to about 16 colours or fewer, keep inner outlines from breaking the outer shape, and keep backgrounds quieter than anything interactive. Shift hue along a ramp (cool shadows, warm lights). [C] | https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-make-pixel-art-that-reads-clearly · https://pixnote.net/en/learn/color-palette · https://alaingalvan.medium.com/pixel-art-design-for-game-dev-32d22c83a296 |
| **Colour never carries a meaning alone**: pair each colour with shape, icon or motion. Players complain when two team colours look alike. [A/C] | https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/?p=2876 · https://warzone.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/mod/pages/7635546/Faction+Team+Player+Colours · https://forums.ageofempires.com/t/please-remove-or-change-the-new-green-color/286022 |

### Principles extracted

13. **One dominant shape per role.** Each role owns one silhouette cue no other role uses: a long horizontal spout for tanks, a peaked roof for carriers, open upward mouths for artillery, negative space under the body for walkers, a pendulum or arm for support, the tallest gilded point for command. Infantry castes differ in mass and head-gear, not in colour alone.
14. **Three reading distances.** In this game a unit is about 0.31 arena units per texel at the horizon (z 1400), 0.62 at z 700 and 1.09 at fire range (z 400): `texel(z) × 1.25 = 435 / z`. Design so that:
    - the *mass and the warm-light pattern* read at the horizon;
    - the *role silhouette* reads mid-field;
    - *caste ornament* reads only near.
15. **Value first, then the warm accent.** Hulls stay in the existing `demon_ramp` (luma 0.06–0.40). The warm accent is the brightest thing on the unit and always means "eye, weapon or telegraph". Rank gilding is darker than the warm accent and never blinks.
16. **Motion is a signature.** Each role moves its own way: tracks crawl, walkers step and bob, infantry waddle, banners flutter, censers swing. The motion repeats the silhouette's message.
17. **Colour coding is relative.** Allies own cyan, teal and grey-blue. The swarm owns its four species hues in the air. The ground army owns dark sector-tinted stone with warm light and dull gold rank. Never let a hull hue approach ally cyan.

## 4. How the principles map onto this project

| Principle | Where it lands |
| --- | --- |
| Spout rule (1) | Every demon weapon fires from a carved mouth fed by a glowing back-channel. The channel filling is the attack telegraph (style guide, "Pillar 1") |
| Architecture as anatomy (3) | Buttress legs, pinnacle horns, rose-window launcher, belfry tower, reliquary carrier (roster) |
| Lodge castes (4, 9) | Rubble → Ashlar → Tracery → Spire. Rank shows in ornament height and gilt (style guide, "Castes") |
| Carved tech (7, 8) | Stone bodies with faces on iron frames. Damage cracks the stone with glowing seams and never draws blood (matches the existing gargoyle damage rule) |
| Familiar objects scaled up (10) | Bell, censer, hod and cistern support units |
| Dark stone, red accents (11, 15) | Existing `demon_ramp`, `demon_steel` and `demon_warm`, plus a new non-blinking `demon_gilt` ramp for rank |
| One shape per role, three distances (13, 14) | Silhouette table and the horizon, mid and near reading rules in the style guide |
| Corruption gradient | Krybor captured and defaced human tech, Ringfall salvage stitched with stone, Ember kiln-forged basalt, Hollow full cathedral masonry, Carrier obsidian elite. This matches the existing "darker and more demonic deeper into the campaign" |

## 5. Originality check

- **Names** come from architecture and masonry (crocket, tympanum, reliquary, carillon, buttress, chapterhouse, thurible, hod, cistern, corbel, ashlar, narthex, rubble, knell, prior). None is a franchise unit name. Search before shipping any new name; the rule is no name that matches a well-known game unit.
- **Shapes**: no unit is a re-skinned franchise unit. Each borrows its shape from a building part, not from a game. The swarm-caste and daemon-engine findings are used only as abstract structure (caste counts, leader relays, a machine with a face).
- **Prompts** name no franchise, studio or artist, positive or negative.
