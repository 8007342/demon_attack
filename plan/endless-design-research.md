# Endless progression, readability, and an immediate score board

Primary references consulted 2026-10-20. The implementation choices below are our application of the references, not prescribed numeric settings from those sources.

Valve's [The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf) describes replayability and dramatic pacing as explicit AI goals, using an intensity estimate and population modulation to alternate pressure and recovery. Apply that pacing principle without introducing hidden adaptive scoring advantages: make each successive Demon Attack loop deterministically harder, with bounded movement/bullet speeds, additional evolution and elite patterns, and clear recovery windows between waves. The next loop should demand better decisions rather than indefinitely reducing reaction time. Add pattern combinations gradually; avoid raising every difficulty variable at once. Record a loop number so progression remains understandable.

Riot's [VALORANT Shaders and Gameplay Clarity](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity) explains treating visual clarity as a gameplay requirement. Applied here: separate enemies, friendly fire, hostile projectiles, pickups, and decorative scenery by silhouette and brightness. A predatory demon needs a visible wind-up and committed target before its lunge; preserve that warning in advanced loops. Molting and angry states should alter form or animation, with color as reinforcement. Keep moons, ringed planets, and ruins dim enough that they never compete with a hostile shot.

Valve's [Portal postmortem: Integrating Narrative and Design](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_PortalPostMortem.pdf) presents embedding exposition in the environment. Applied here: let a voyage reveal more of Krybor through damaged orbital structures, alien beacons, debris, and changing celestial alignments. Repeated locations can acquire new environmental details each loop. Short sector labels can anchor those clues; avoid explanatory paragraphs during combat. The world should become stranger as enemies evolve while the moving hazards retain their familiar readable shapes.

The [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/) recommend straightforward menus, readable text, stationary interactive targets, and starting play without traversing multiple menu layers. Applied here: put the complete Top10 directly on the landing page, alongside the immediate Start action. On phones the board can extend below the hero in the same page scroll; it should never require a separate menu button. Loading, empty, and failed leaderboard states stay within the board, leaving Start available. Offer an inline retry after failure. After a successful three-wheel initials submission, return to the landing board with the submitted result and a brief acknowledgment. This preserves the restricted initials model and gives useful feedback without adding another destination screen.

Between waves, keep ship controls and pickup collection active while threats are suspended. A safe salvage interval should feel like earned recovery rather than input loss. A common `canPilot` simulation predicate should govern both keyboard and controller input so their behavior cannot drift between combat and recovery states.
