# Demon Attack — visual and musical style guide

## Direction and implemented asset contract

Original pixel artwork honors early-console clarity with animated winged demons, cosmic scenery, and expressive damage states. Ordinary enemies are living creatures: horns, membrane wings, teeth, eyes, and tails. The carrier alone combines a broad flying hull with demonic features and a mechanical sliding hatch. Preserve hard pixel edges and nearest-neighbor filtering. Keep threats readable against darker scenery; do not bake interface text into images.

The authoritative production atlas layout is [sprite-atlas.md](sprite-atlas.md), generated by `tools/generate_sprites.py` into `assets/images/combat.png`. It is a transparent 512 × 1280 image, twenty rows and eight columns of 64 × 64 cells, authored on a logical 32 × 32 grid enlarged exactly 2×. Do not substitute a differently sized concept sheet into the renderer.

| Row | Current content |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Four teal demon wing frames, followed by four angry variants |
| 1 | Four violet armored wing frames, followed by angry variants |
| 2 | Four coral predator wing frames, followed by angry variants |
| 3 | Four gold crowned elder wing frames, followed by angry variants |
| 4 | Four player exhaust frames; spread, rapid, shield, heart icons |
| 5 | Eight explosion frames |
| 6 | Four carrier transformation forms; four pickup badges |
| 7 | Four console tile variants; four spark frames |
| 8–19 | Three intermediate molts per species, each with four normal and four highlighted wing frames |

The shield, sliding door, health meters, and core vulnerability are renderer overlays tied to simulation state. The creatures have normal, three intermediate damaged silhouettes, and angry appearances. Rows8–19 provide the intermediate molts; each nonlethal hit updates the rendering lookup. Four carrier forms are available; later cycles unlock the later forms.

Current combat palette: midnight outline #100E2C; teal #39DFCC; violet #A888FF; coral #FF786C; gold #EFBD57; ivory highlights and red angry eyes. Space uses near-black blue and purple. Friendly shots are cyan; hostile shots use warm red/orange. Shapes and motion reinforce color. Render all new art at native scale before judging enlarged previews. Procedural production sprites and generated illustration/concept assets have distinct roles; illustrations do not replace the atlas automatically.

## Level and screen art direction

| Level | Visual identity |
| --- | --- |
| KRYBOR | Violet night, rocky horizon, pale rising moon |
| RINGFALL | Teal night, ringed planet and thin cloud bands |
| EMBER MOON | Crimson-violet horizon with contrasting moons |
| THE HOLLOW | Amber eclipse and distant carrier silhouette |
| THE CARRIER | Black-purple occultation; bright shield and core |
| Victory | Warm gold horizon accents and restrained sparks |
| Title / Top 10 | Quiet stars, cyan emblem, gold score accents |

The intended scenery has far stars, slower planet layers, and a closer horizon, with planets and moons rising or setting as the game advances. Background luminance stays below enemy and projectile luminance. Smooth palette and planetary movement between levels is the target; exact layer speeds and transition timing are renderer tuning, not simulation defaults. Reduced-motion support should remove shake and decorative motion without hiding warning cues.

Title centers an original horned demon emblem and the Demon Attack wordmark, with Start, Top 10, sound, and short controls guidance. The game HUD displays score, level/cycle, lives, and current power; boss meters appear during the carrier encounter. Touch controls occupy a recessed futuristic laptop-style trackpad beneath the arena, with cyan corner lights, pixel circuit traces, and a visible drag indicator. Keep fingers away from combat. Real interface text remains legible and accessible; no fake labels baked into generated art.

Top 10 uses aligned rank, three-character initials, and score columns. Initial entry offers three rotating wheels with A–Z and 0–9, clear upper/lower controls, a highlighted middle selection, and Submit. Avoid a freeform text field. Game-over and victory keep a darkened arena behind their overlays. Review phone portrait, narrow desktop, and wide desktop without horizontal overflow.

## Current MIDI score and synthesizer

`web/audio/generate-midi.py` composes original Standard MIDI Format 0 files in `web/audio/midi/`. `web/audio/demon-audio.js` parses their note events and plays them through a local Web Audio synthesizer. These are actual MIDI sequences, not prerecorded soundtrack files. The current score deliberately shares **120 BPM** and a four-beat bar across every mode, allowing transitions at the next bar boundary while previous note releases finish. Increasing note density and orchestration supplies tension instead of changing tempo.

| Cue | Current implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Menu | Sparse sine-pad motif; 32 beats |
| Levels 1–4 | Distinct 32-beat scores with increasing melodic subdivisions; percussion from LV2 |
| Boss phases 1–4 | Four increasingly dense 32-beat scores; each has a denser later-cycle version |
| Victory | Eight-beat major fanfare; final cadence continues until travel |
| Level clear | Immediate eight-beat major flourish with a 90 ms music fade |
| Travel | Sixteen-beat suspended arpeggio bridge on the common musical grid |
| Game over | Eight-beat descending cue and dark chord, non-looping |

The carrier has up to four visual forms, each with its own increasing MIDI intensity. Voices use square lead, sawtooth bass, sine pad, and triangle percussion. A shared audio-clock scheduler runs with lookahead and asynchronous cue loading; a request counter rejects stale loads. Track changes queue to the next bar; level-clear rewards respond immediately with a short music-only crossfade. Firing, hits, explosions, power collection, and life loss use short separate synth effects.

Visiting the page remains silent until a user gesture unlocks browser audio. Start and sound controls should support that gesture deliberately. Muting ramps the master gain. Pause suspends the audio context; Resume restarts scheduling. The current bridge also responds to visibility changes. Confirm that its behavior remains aligned with the application's deliberate pause/resume controls.

Future refinements: stem crossfades, per-mode tempo ramps, music/effects balance controls, sound preference persistence, and stronger sound-effect voice limiting. These are intended improvements, not claims about the current synthesizer.

## Review gates

At native scale every demon species, angry state, player, and pickup must be recognizable, with no screenshot or original-game sprite reused. Inspect extracted alpha edges and atlas frames; generated concept sheets require review before becoming production assets. Enemy bullets must remain visible on every background. Musical loops should have no silent boundary, old voices should release cleanly, state changes must not restore stale cues, and first visit must stay silent until interaction. Browser checks should cover music through a complete boss transformation, victory, next cycle, pause, resume, and muted play.

## Multiwave and cinematic presentation

LV1–2 have three waves; LV3–4 have four. Intermediate-wave banners last 1.4 s and retain the level’s musical identity. The final wave instead leads to a 4 s level-clear scene and 4 s travel scene before the next level’s 2.3 s introduction. Show travel with the same cosmic palette, advancing the horizon / planetary composition rather than exposing combat. The carrier’s defeat has a distinct 5.5 s triumph, then 4 s travel before the next cycle. Keep score and lives legible, freeze power timers, suppress damage and auto-fire, and retain exact visual progress while paused. Use the model’s normalized transitionProgress to drive these scenes so animation and phase timing agree.

## Combined power glyphs

Power status uses transparent pixel-grid layers rather than opaque pickup tiles: spread cyan #70F5D5 arrows, rapid gold #F4CA79 central lightning, shield violet #A888FF outer enclosure. Layers combine in one badge without hiding the side arrows or shield. Display individual names, countdowns and timer bars beside the mark inside the controller panel, leaving the arena unobstructed. Losing one modifier removes only its visual layer and row.
