# Sprite atlas contract (64-grid)

This is the authoritative layout of every combat sprite sheet. Creatures are authored on a **64 × 64 logical-pixel grid** for a 16-bit level of detail, drawn at the **same on-screen size and hitbox footprint** as always, as crisp nearest-neighbour pixel art with a bounded palette, hard edges and discrete authored frames. No smooth vector creatures, gradients or continuously bent shapes.

Since the 2026-11 gargoyle round, ordinary creatures and sentinels are **demonic gargoyles, not birds**: stony, leathery hide, heavy shoulders and a hunched torso, curled horns, a snarling fanged maw, glowing eyes, clawed hands and feet, a barbed tail and bat membranes stretched between finger bones with torn edges. They are seen in a **3/4 flying pose** (camera tilted 34° above the creature, body leaning toward the player) and every flight frame exists for banking left, neutral and banking right.

All four sheets are **stored 1×**: one PNG pixel is one logical pixel. Render with `FilterQuality.none` and `isAntiAlias = false`. Coordinates are zero-based `(row, column)`. Lookups live in `lib/game/sprite_atlas.dart` (combat, with the motion state in `lib/game/creature_motion.dart`), `lib/game/ship_painter.dart` (ship) and `lib/game/scene.dart` (sentinel, carrier). Every sheet is original: generated by the deterministic tools below, with optional AI-derived shading detail that never changes a silhouette (see [asset-pipeline.md](asset-pipeline.md#sprites)). No sprite, screenshot or trace of the 1982 game or any other game is used.

## Palette

Every packed cell is snapped to it and alpha is binarised (0/255). ## The gargoyle rig

`tools/gargoyle_rig.py` is a small deterministic 3-D rasteriser. A species is a set of volumes (ellipsoids and tapered sphere chains: pelvis, belly plate, chest, back hump, shoulders, neck, head, brow, muzzle, jaw, ears, horns, arms with three-clawed hands, legs with three-toed talons, a barbed tail) plus two bat wings (humerus, forearm, thumb claw, three finger bones and five membrane panels per wing, with a scalloped, notched trailing edge). A frame poses the rig (wing joint angles, whole-body lift, tail sway, leg swing, jaw, head turn), turns it for its direction (yaw ±38°, roll ±12°), tilts it by the fixed camera pitch (34°; dives 52°) and rasterises it orthographically at pixel centres with a z-buffer. Shading is a quantised top-left-front key light onto each material's four-tone ramp (membrane undersides use the two darker tones, so the upstroke reads as the wing turning over); stray single tone pixels are cleaned up; a dark contour is drawn on the far side of every depth step between parts; and a 1 px `gink` outline closes the silhouette. Faces, rank marks, weapon glows and damage fissures are pixel decals pinned to 3-D anchor points and drawn only where their own part (or material) is visible, so they turn, hide behind a wing and stay on the same anatomical side in every direction. A right-banking frame is therefore a real render, not a mirror.

Readability caps: no feature thinner than 1 logical px against the outline; eyes are 2 × 2 with a 1 px bright core under a dark brow; the maw is 5–7 px wide with fangs; insignia stay within about 5 × 5 px; detail textures never cover eyes, insignia or plates.

| base + 8 | Elite armour overlays: column `direction × 4 + level` (0–11), dive overlays 12–15; level 0–3 = armour 4 → 1 |

**Flight set (10 frames per direction).** 0–3 **downstroke**: extended wings drive down and the whole body lifts (lift 0, −1, −2, −2 px); 4–7 **upstroke**: the wrists lead, the wings fold (fold up to 50 %, span visibly narrower) and rise while the body drops (lift −1, 0, +1, +1); 8–9 **glide**: wings held spread with a slight dihedral, legs trailing (lift 0, +1). The lifts are `FLAP_LIFT + GLIDE_LIFT` in the generator and `CombatAtlas.flightLift` in Dart. The neutral set is the **hover/idle cycle**. In the banking sets the body turns (yaw ±38°) and leans (roll ±12°), the head leads 8° further into the turn, the inner wing beats shallower and folds more while the outer wing reaches further, and the tail trails outward.

**Damage.** Molts 1–3 progressively punch membrane holes and bite the trailing edges (more on the creature's port wing), chip and then snap horn tips (port first), open glowing orange fissures across the stony hide (one, three, then five, with a glowing core on molt 3), remove the coral stinger (molt 2) and the coral tail (molt 3), and erode the gold crown tines. Every damage variant exists for every direction and flight frame, so there is no overlay compositing. Selection: `variant = maxHp − hp` (clamped 0–3); `hp == 1 && maxHp > 1` uses the angry row.

**Hit flash.** Authored highlighted frames (every ramp stepped toward white, outline and fissure glows kept) for each variant, direction and even flap pose; glide frames use pose 2's flash. Anticipation and fire frames keep the white tint as before; dives have their own two flash frames.

**Death** (0.56 s, 8 frames): flash; glowing seams; then the stone body breaks into 5 × 5 px chunks that tumble outward and fall, glowing then cooling to grey, around a core burst, with dust puffs at the end. The scene plays it where an ordinary demon leaves the simulation with `hp <= 0` (`CreaturePresentation`).

**Elites** add authored armour from the same rig, kept as the pixels the armour adds over the unarmoured body (wings omitted, so plates never paint over a wing): level 4 a steel gorget and gold-studded pauldrons, level 3 three gold crest spikes, level 2 side helm plates, level 1 the gold double-chevron rank mark on the brow, which every elite keeps. Each hit sheds one level (`level = (4 − hp).clamp(0, 3)`). The overlay is drawn at the creature's position offset by the frame's body lift (`lift × size / 64` arena units: a whole logical pixel, so it stays on the pixel grid). The dotted rank halo stays a scene effect.

### Frame selection

`CombatAtlas.creature(d, clock, fireAge:, motion:)` returns the cell, the tint flag, the elite overlay cell and the lift. Presentation only: it reads the model and never changes it. Priority:

1. **Dive warning** (`telegraph > 0`): anticipation 0–3 at 12 fps.
2. **Dive** (`dive > 0`): the variant's dive pair at 8 fps (dive flash while `flash > 0`).
3. **Weapon fire** (`fireAge < 0.28 s`, from `CreaturePresentation`): the fire or angry-fire columns.
4. **Flight**: direction block × damage variant × flight frame from the creature's `CreatureMotion`; the flash slot while `flash > 0`.

`CreatureMotion` (`lib/game/creature_motion.dart`, owned by `CreaturePresentation`, updated once per scene update and frozen while paused) measures velocity from the creature's successive positions, smooths it (time constant 1/12 s) and turns it into states:

| State | Enter | Leave | Hold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Banking left / right | `vx ≤ −55` / `vx ≥ 55` units/s (also flips directly when the other side's enter threshold is crossed) | `|vx| < 24` | ≥ 0.14 s |
| Glide | `vy > 70` (sinking) | `vy ≤ 32` | ≥ 0.28 s |

The stroke phase advances at 2 strokes/s (1.3× while climbing, `vy < −30`) and is offset per creature by its seed. The downstroke takes 62 % of the stroke while climbing, 50 % in level flight and 38 % while sinking (`vy > 25`), so rising creatures visibly drive their wings down and sinking ones linger in the folded upstroke. Jumps over 60 units in one update (respawns, formation resets) are ignored. Without tracker state (a single painted frame), the direction falls back to the simulation's smoothed `bank` (±0.45) and the stroke to the clock. The scene no longer rotates or squashes ordinary creatures for banking: the authored frames carry it.

Every cell is a full layer centred on the ship (logical (32, 32) = ship centre), drawn at 48 × 48 arena units, so 1 logical px = 0.75 units. Module rows 1, 2, 4, 5 share columns: 0–2 wear fresh / worn (scorch pixels) / critical (glowing seams); 3–5 recoil (kick 2 px with breech heat, kick 1 px, settle); 6–9 muzzle flash; 10–13 deploy transition (wireframe sparkle, half-built, metal flash, settled); 14 the spent module centred for the tumbling `FallingPart`; 15 heat overlay flickered over critical wear.

**Firing presentation (no gameplay change).** No wing module: the nose cannon flashes (`model.noseFlash`). A wing module (powerful first, then rapid) fits wing cannons that fire the primary shot alternately left, right, left; only the side that fired plays recoil and muzzle frames, from `model.wingRecoil[side]` over `ArcadeModel.wingRecoilDuration` (0.09 s): recoil column `3/4/5` at > 66 / > 33 / > 0 % remaining, flash frame `⌊(1 − remaining) × 4⌋`. Spread and large modules recoil and flash on every volley. A power collected within the last 0.24 s plays its deploy frames.

**Muzzles are gameplay anchors.** `ship-weapons.json` carries `wing_muzzles` (rapid (−15.5, −10), powerful (−17, −13)) and `nose_muzzle` (0, −10) in arena units, the values the research branch established in `ship-parts.json`. The scene copies them into `model.wingMuzzles` at load exactly as before, so projectiles spawn where they did. The art is built around these points (`generate_ship_sprites.py` `to_px`). Change them only together with `test/wing_cannons_test.dart`.

## Sentinel and effects atlas: `sentinel-effects.png`

Each 128 × 64 cell is the logical grid; render at 128 × 64 units, or ×0.5 for compact effects. Row meanings are unchanged by the gargoyle redesign, so renderer lookups did not change.

The sentinels are large gargoyles from the same rig (scale 1.4, origin (64, 30), pitch 30°): the shield sentinel is a horned violet **warden** with a steel breastplate and violet crescent; the armoured sentinel a crowned gold **colossus** with a ruby sunburst. Their bat wings are half as long again as an ordinary creature's, so the level pose spans almost the whole cell. Their weapon is the maw.

Warnings stay on the creature: no arrows or detached marks. The fairness timings of the last stand (0.9 s tell, 600/540/630 units/s rush) are simulation values and unchanged; rows 6/7 only draw them.
