# Demon Attack — game design and current tuning

This original remake uses winged demons, a horizontally moving defender, permanent auto-fire, four multi-wave sectors and a low-flying brood carrier. A campaign ends after five carrier victories. Artwork and music are original. Historical references belong in the research document; the values here describe the implementation and remain playtesting defaults.

## Campaign and difficulty

A route visits Krybor, Ringfall, Ember Moon, The Hollow, and a carrier. Five routes progressively approach the brood base. Internal route indices are zero-based; players see destination names instead of level/cycle numbers.

Normal is available initially. Three game overs before any boss victory permanently unlock Easy on that browser. Any boss victory records that a boss has been beaten; a Normal boss victory permanently unlocks Hard and Easy. Older history with Hard unlocked also exposes Easy. Player movement and firing are identical across modes. Easy adjusts hostile actors rather than slowing the whole simulation.

Every boss awards one medal and one life, once. Difficulty determines the medal design; Hard medals are most elaborate. After the falling-carrier and fleet-recognition scene, travel leads to the next named destination. The fifth carrier ends the campaign after its reward sequence. Remaining lives include the final +1UP. Active time advances during combat only; pauses, introductions, salvage and cinematic travel do not count.

## Simulation and progression

The arena is 360 × 520 logical units; ship y=477. An interactive **Stylized Starfighter Cockpit Control Panel** sits beneath it, combining the **Starfighter Weapon/Shield Telemetry Schematic** with the rotating **Steering Yoke & Gun Angle Gimbal**. Dragging the cockpit yoke/panel, pressing arrow keys, or tilting the device (**calibrated 3D device tilt** via `screenRoll` with circular-mean neutral posture calibration and jump-free multi-touch release rebasing) steers the starfighter and couples horizontal thrust with **interim turret gun vectoring angles (`±6°, ±12°, ±18°, ±24°`)**. The ship approaches its target with smooth acceleration/deceleration inertia (`shipVx` up to ±330 units/s, accelerating at 2200 units/s², decelerating at 1800 units/s², and reversing at 2800 units/s²), clamped to x=18–342, while tilting into a continuous banking pose (`shipBank` in `[-1.0, 1.0]`). Coupled steering and turret gun vectoring allow fired projectiles to vector through discrete interim shooting angles (`0°, ±6°, ±12°, ±18°, ±24°`) in addition to strategic **sling shooting**: fired projectiles inherit 38% of the ship's horizontal velocity (`vx = shipVx × 0.38`, plus Spread's lateral offset and interim gun vector angle when active) and gain up to +18% vertical speed at full strafe (`vy = -390 × (1 + 0.18 × |shipVx|/330)`), letting skilled pilots curve and angle shots around carrier hull edges or into moving targets. Baseline fire interval is 0.23 s; bullets travel upward at 390 units/s at rest. The simulation uses 60 fixed steps/s and clamps a single update's elapsed time to 0.1 s.

Starting a new game launches a **3-Beat Opening Cockpit Panel Zoom-Out** transition:
1. **Beat 1 (Cockpit Ignition)**: Tight framing on the bottom Cockpit Control Panel as the weapon/shield telemetry schematic and steering yoke boot up and calibrate.
2. **Beat 2 (Continuous Zoom-Out & Launch)**: Motivated continuous camera pullback from the cockpit panel into the live `360 × 520` arena viewport without fading to black, as the starfighter's twin thrusters ignite onto `y = 477`.
3. **Beat 3 (Horizon Reveal & Squadron Arrival)**: Sector parallax stabilizes and the opening demon squadron sweeps in after the arrival hint card.

One unprotected hit removes a life and starts the **life-loss sequence** (`lib/game/life_loss.dart`). First the ship breaks apart in a 0.7 s multi-frame pixel explosion, with hull shards and its fitted modules flying. Then comes a 1.6 s **regroup**: hostile shots fizzle out, ground missiles in flight end, dives pull out and the swarm returns to formation. No hostile fire, dive, reinforcement or ground launch starts, and nothing can hit the player. Finally the reserve ship flies up from the bottom edge in 0.55 s with 2 s of blinking protection, and play resumes. Module timers freeze while the ship is away; lives, scoring and the loadout rules are unchanged. Events: `playerDestroyed`, `regroupStart` and `playerRespawn`. Every dive tell is preceded by a 0.5 s `divePrep` beat, so the demons can radio first. Losing the final life triggers the **Last-Life-Lost Defeat Celebration & Skyward Camera Pan to High Scores**: the player ship breaks apart in a multi-stage explosion, surviving ordinary demons, Sentinels, or the Brood Dreadnought perform a post-mortem **enemy victory celebration** (synchronized figure-8/chevron victory swarms, Sentinel wing salutes, or Carrier mandible/thruster victory patrols), and the camera pans skyward into the upper starfield where the **High Scores (Top 10) & 3-Wheel Initials Entry** console settles into view. The initial ship has 2 s protection. Pause freezes combat, powers and transition clocks. Each sector starts with a 4.5 s illustrated hint introduction (including the `sling_shot` arrival hint teaching banked diagonal shots). Hints advance automatically with no guide or extra buttons; ordinary pause still freezes them. Initial swarms stay offscreen until the hint clears, then sweep in along curved entrance trajectories (`enterStyle` 0..3: sweeping side arcs, top spiral loops, figure-8 descents, and split pincers) that blend into active formation coordinates via a quintic smootherstep (`6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3`) envelope with zero position or velocity snap. Initially Krybor and Ringfall have three waves, Ember Moon and The Hollow four. Each later route adds one wave per sector, capped at three extra waves. Clearing an intermediate wave gives a 1.733 s introduction. The sector's final wave gives a protected 2 s salvage window, a 4.333 s victory scene and 4.333 s travel, accompanied by **pictorial storyboard interludes** illustrating campaign milestones. A boss victory uses a 5.833 s reward sequence.

The bounded ordinary roster is `round((8 + 2×(sector−1) + wave−1 + route) × density)`, with density 0.8/1/1.2 and caps 20/24/28 for Easy/Normal/Hard. One shared blueprint selects species and health for opening enemies, reinforcements and dedicated escorts. Ordinary HP is `min(5, 1 + floor(kind/2) + floor(route/2) + elite)`: only two route evolution steps across the campaign; a reinforcement ordinal never adds HP. Potential elites occur every tenth ordinary birth, with a whole-wave birth budget 0 on the first route and 1/2/3 later. Fresh arrivals never renew this budget or all become elite.

Normal and Hard each have two finite reinforcement groups; Easy has none on the first route and one later. Group size is `round((4 + floor(route/2)) × density) + min(groupIndex,1)`: the second adds just one creature. The first casualty can request an arrival after 0.6 seconds; subsequent groups use a 1.2-second cooldown and two casualties, with a 4.5-second fallback. Admission remains bounded by initial roster plus the largest ordinary group, so timers cannot flood an already crowded fight. Existing interwave introductions and salvage/travel scenes retain breathing room.

Each sentinel still brings its own fresh escort, independent of ordinary groups: size 12/13/14 plus up to two later-route members. Escorts use the same parent health/species/elite budget. Escort admission allows initial roster plus escort size; absolute active population stays capped at 61. These events create no checkpoints. See [difficulty-calibration.md](difficulty-calibration.md) and [research-source-ledger.md](research-source-ledger.md) for primary research, precise formulas, hit-count tables and the before/after audit.

Flocks combine individual sinusoidal formation anchors with home-tethered Craig Reynolds Boids steering (local neighbor **Separation** within 34 units, **Cohesion/Alignment** within 68 units, and incoming-shot lateral **Evade** jinks within `dx < 28, 0 < dy < 95`, bounded to `±22` horizontal and `±10` vertical offsets) plus an embedded 244-parameter (`10→16→4`) offline synthetic-imitation JEV action prior (`SwarmPolicy` in `lib/game/swarm_policy.dart`, documented in [policy.md](policy.md), achieving 96.98% held-out teacher agreement across `['hold', 'evade_left', 'evade_right', 'dive_commit']`). Simultaneous diving attackers are capped (`maxConcurrentDives = 2 + min(route, 2)`). Dive warnings sample the player's x once and include a brief upward pre-dive crest (`-16 units/s`) with a wing-flare glint; dives do not continuously track the player. Multi-hit creatures visibly molt, become angry at their final HP, and telegraph an attack. Scarce elites add one hull HP, faster dives and targeted fans within the shared whole-wave budget. Naturally spawned hostile bullets are capped at 120.

All diving creatures project a **3-tier atmospheric re-entry plasma fire shield (`diveShield` / `diveShieldMax`)** during active dives, visually and chromatically distinct from **Technological Force Shields**:
- **Atmospheric Re-Entry Plasma Fire (Dive Envelopes)**: Uses a strictly **thermal blackbody gradient** (blinding white stagnation core -> incandescent solar yellow -> superheated ablation orange -> deep crimson/charcoal smoke trail with turbulent pixel jitter and shedding embers; **zero cyan/violet bands**):
  1. **Small creatures** (1–2 HP lower-tier ordinary demons): project a **1-HP "lil'" thermal re-entry bow-shock shield** (`diveShieldMax = 1`) when diving, popped by 1 baseline shot or pierced in a single shot by 2-damage `Powerful`.
  2. **Not-so-small creatures** (elites and tougher/higher-species ordinary demons, `elite || kind >= 2 || maxHp >= 3`): project a **not-so-small (2–3 HP medium) ablative re-entry plasma shield** (`diveShieldMax = 2..3`) with a wider double-layered thermal bow-shock envelope.
  3. **Large creatures (Sentinels)**: project a **large V-shaped atmospheric re-entry plasma cone** (`diveShieldMax = 4 + floor(route/2)`) when diving, and **a sentinel's dive attack/shield can ONLY be broken when the player has active offensive powerups** (unpowered shots deflect off the re-entry shield without breaking it). Shattering a sentinel's dive shield mid-dive triggers **Plasma Shatter** (a 0.45 s mid-air stun, immediate dive-momentum cancellation, and a `+250 × (route + 1)` score bonus), and destroying a sentinel during its dive awards `1.5×` kill points.
- **Technological Force Shields (Player Shield, Sentinel Hover Shield & Carrier Energy Barrier)**: Use cool, structured **geometric cyan/teal/violet hex & segmented pixel barriers** without smoke trails or thermal ember shedding.

After the first boss victory only, scarce wide line-wing sentinels descend slowly after late-wave swarm arrivals, hover above their protective screen, then anticipate a fast dive with warning sprite poses, with at most one special enemy in a generated flock. Normal and Hard gain additional later-wave eligibility on later routes. Shield sentinels have `9 + 2×min(route,4)` hull HP plus separately depleted shield/armor; armored sentinels have `12 + 2×min(route,4)` hull HP plus their defenses, appearing from route index 2 in Normal and Hard. They show their actual shield/armor from first sight, evade approaching fire with bounded lateral moves, fire distinct faster bullets, survive repeated attacks and dives, and break apart in a distinct wing-and-ember defeat animation. A minority of sentinels (15% Easy, 25% Normal/Hard, selected once on spawn) make a last stand at 25% health or less: a subtle 0.9-second climbing tell confined to the creature locks the player position, followed by a nonhoming 600-unit/s Normal sacrifice rush (540 Easy, 630 Hard, following mode hostile-rate tuning). Impact or escape destroys them without kill rewards; shooting one still earns its normal reward. Dedicated raised-wing and folded-wing sprite frames distinguish this attack. Ordinary sprite forms retain their established behavior.

## Boss

The **Brood Dreadnought Carrier** is rendered as a multi-part sprite hierarchy on a **`184 × 120` logical pixel dreadnought carapace** across **4 evolution forms** (`Carrier`, `Brood Ark`, `Siege Carrier`, `Brood Flagship`), fitted with **articulated siege mandibles**, **dorsal retro-thrusters**, **tracking ventral dual-turrets**, **3 mechanical launch bay shutters**, and **progressive fracture overlays**.
- **Arrival ("Coming-In") Choreography**: The dreadnought enters via **orbital retro-burn deceleration** with flaring dorsal retro-thrusters, **gravitational dust ripples** across the sector backdrop, **mandible deployment**, and warning **klaxon telemetry** before locking into its cruise sweep near `y = 205` with a 12-unit bob and an 80-unit horizontal sweep.
- **Shield, Apertures & Endurance**: Each encounter selects a seeded random starting direction/phase; damage accelerates movement and intensifies pixel rage eyes. To match early boss challenge to emerging player skill, Route 0 and Route 1 carrier endurance and attack/minion cadence are dialed down: Normal/Hard shield HP is **48** on Route 0, **64** on Route 1, and `72 + route×18` on Routes 2–4; core HP is **16** on Route 0, **20** on Route 1, and `24 + route×10` on Routes 2–4. Easy scales both by 0.8 and rounds. The technological force field (cool cyan/teal/violet geometric hex/segmented barrier) spans 220 units, wider than the 184-unit hull, at every remaining strength. Its symmetric lower egg contracts in depth from 75 toward 43 arena units as energy falls, and actual shot impacts follow that surface. It disappears only at zero energy; weapons retain their normal damage. After the shield breaks, the carrier exposes **three mechanical launch bay shutters during non-final forms (`phase < formCount - 1`)**: the primary center hatch (`|dx| < 24` when `door >= 0.65`, dealing 100% shot damage) plus **Left and Right auxiliary side hatches** (`sideDoorLeft` and `sideDoorRight` at `dx ≈ ±52`, width `±18`, opening out of phase when `sideDoor >= 0.55` and transmitting `0.75×` rounded damage, minimum 1). When the carrier enters its **final form (`phase == formCount - 1`)**, both auxiliary side hatches seal shut (`0.0`) and core damage requires a shot through the center aperture while `door >= 0.65`. Closed hull blocks shots. Shield, belly and center hatch share a centerline.
- **Destruction ("Going-Down") Choreography**: Depleting the core triggers a **sequential multi-part chain-reaction meltdown** propagating from the outer **wing pylons -> auxiliary bays -> central brood core**, sending the dreadnought into a **listing hull descent** with progressive fracture overlays, shedding armor plates, sparks, and dense pixel smoke columns before the fleet-recognition and medal scene.

The boss releases flocks when fewer than ten minions are present, with no more than ten simultaneous minions. Minion HP grows to three. They replenish while the core remains reachable; clearing them is not required to defeat the carrier. The boss also launches a downward fan. Boss score is `5000×(route+1)`; clearing minions at victory awards no extra minion points. Easy's enemy-points multiplier applies to enemy eliminations. Continuous boss flocks remain a possible source of score farming in this trust-based arcade leaderboard.

## Powers and hearts

Enemy eliminations award points once; damage alone earns none. Ordinary base points are `(100 + kind×75 + (maxHp−1)×50)×(route+1)`, then apply difficulty scaling. Kill count persists through sectors and routes. Every eighth kill drops the next power in a repeating spread → rapid → shield → powerful → large sequence.

Powers combine across two exclusive offensive groups and an independent Shield, each with a nine-second timer. Rapid and Powerful replace each other; Spread and Large replace each other. A pickup refreshes its own type and removes only its same-group alternative. Spread adds two angled shots; Rapid changes the fire interval to 0.105 s. Powerful doubles damage at ordinary cadence with a distinctive shot. Large creates a bounded pixel blast on impact that damages nearby demons; it can reach the carrier core only with shield depleted and door open. Powerful, Large and Shield can combine. Shield absorbs one collision, consumes itself and grants 1 s protection. Life loss preserves offensive powers. Pause and cinematic phases freeze power expiry. Replaced or expired modules are jettisoned as tumbling debris that cannot be collected; it keeps falling through salvage, victory and travel scenes until it leaves the screen. Pickups normally fall at 64 units/s, increasing to 144 during the final salvage window.

Easy and Normal independently sample rare heart-piece drops alongside powers. Four complementary bottom-left/bottom-right/top-left/top-right quarters form a heart and award a life immediately. Three unfinished slots each hold a bitmask of distinct parts. A repeated piece fills the next slot missing that part; three identical quarters reserve all three slots, and a fourth is ignored. Completing a slot clears it for future pieces, with a brief solid-heart flash, explicit +1UP notification and a larger life count; a new piece immediately takes visual precedence over that flash. Slots show individual counts out of four. Hard has no heart drops or collection; bosses provide its only extra lives.

## Ground war

Over sectors 1–4 (never the carrier) demon ground forces drive along the flyover's perspective ground plane, always in the side bands outside the firing lane: Krybor rocket buggies, Ringfall salvage crawlers, Ember artillery beasts and Hollow siege walkers. They appear tiny and fogged at the horizon (z 1400, y ≈ 215), approach at 105 texels/s, and fire only from the near ground (z 400 → 205, y ≈ 323 → 467) after a 1.1 s telegraph (launcher raise, then a pulsing glow and a warm ground bracket) and a launch flash. Each unit carries 1 missile on Easy and 2 on Normal/Hard (the first Krybor unit always 1). At most 3 missiles fly at once and one unit telegraphs at a time. Player shots and Large blasts pass over ground units.

**Ground missiles** fly a fixed path in 1.7 s ÷ hostile rate to the player's x sampled at launch, popping up and growing from 8 to 36 units. They collide only from the ship's plane (t ≥ 0.9), with a hitbox of 0.4 × their drawn width plus the ship's 9 × 10 half-extents; a warm marker shows where the path crosses the ship plane. A hit is an ordinary hostile hit, which the shield absorbs. Shots cannot destroy missiles: permanent auto-fire would erase them without any player decision and remove both the dodge and the purpose of homing missiles.

**The front** (`GroundFront`, time-based: it runs on the level's combat clock and ignores air waves, so fast and slow players meet the same war). The first unit arrives 5–7 s into Krybor on every difficulty. Arrivals come every 6–8 s ÷ (0.7 + 0.6 × pressure), with at most 1–2 live units (+1 under high pressure), and none while a broken-through unit can still fire. In Krybor and Ringfall every unit is escorted and the allies always destroy it before fire range: quiet background skirmishes the radio still reports. From Ember Moon a unit is escorted with probability = allied strength, and an unescorted or out-gunned unit breaks through (`groundBreakthrough`): occasionally over Ember and regularly over the Hollow, ordered Easy < Normal < Hard and growing with route. **Allied strength** (0.2–1) rises with player help (homing kills +0.08, saves +0.04) and allied wins, falls with retreats and breakthroughs, and settles toward the sector base (1.0/0.95/0.76/0.62 on Normal). **Pressure** (0–1, `groundPressure` at each tenth) rises with live, threatening and broken-through units and level progress, and falls when the allies are ahead. Missiles in flight end at every wave entrance and at salvage; units end at level clear; checkpoints restore without them.

**Homing missiles** are a separate pickup, not a timed module. Collecting one arms a salvo of three that fire automatically, each 0.18 s after the previous one hits. Ground units come first (telegraphing > in range and reloaded soonest > closest); otherwise air targets in the order open carrier core > sentinel > elite > gold > coral > violet > teal; last, the carrier's shield or closed core. One missile destroys a ground unit. Against air targets it is one heavy hit of 3: it kills 1–3 HP demons, strips a sentinel layer by 3, breaks a sentinel's dive shield, and takes 3 off the carrier's shield, or off its core only through an open hatch. The salvo combines with every module and the shield, survives life loss, and refills to three if collected again. Drops are urgent while a breakthrough looms or a broken-through unit can still fire: 30/25/20% per ordinary kill, guaranteed on the next kill after 3/4/5 s, and air-dropped into the lane 3 s later if no kill comes. Otherwise homing missiles are an ordinary pickup at kills 20, 60, 100, … (each module's cadence in the eight-kill rotation), over the carrier too. No homing drop occurs while one is falling or a salvo is armed. The eight-kill rotation and heart drops are unchanged.

**Allies** are human frontier troops, rovers, turrets and walkers in cyan/teal friendly colours. Escorted demon units arrive engaged with one ally, and the pair advances together trading tracers. The ally's cyan fire destroys the demon unit after 2–10 hits (by sector, difficulty and route) at 0.69–1.1 s per shot (by allied strength). The demon's tracer costs the ally 1 of 8 HP every 1.3 s ÷ (0.8 + 0.6 × pressure) ÷ hostile rate. When a unit is predicted to break through, its allies call for help once (`allyUnderAttack`, with a blinking cyan beacon). Allies never collide, never block shots and never die: at 0 HP an ally is totalled and limps to the outer edge, smoking (odd-numbered crews eject in a pod), then leaves (`allyRetreated`). Destroying an engaged ally's attacker saves it (`allySaved`, +250 × route). A sector cleared without any retreat scores +500 × route when allies fought and emits `fleetIntact` (scope level); a campaign without retreats emits `fleetIntact` (scope campaign). Consequences are positive only.

**Round 3 (`openspec/changes/gameplay-round-3`).**
- **Convoy allies:** allies hold station in the near band beside the ship (z 250–302, y ≈ 404–420). They advance up the plane, to at most z 620, only to engage, then fall back. Reinforcements roll in from below.
- **Ground homing:** homing missiles fired at ground units fly a 0.95–1.75 s perspective arc that climbs, arcs out and dives into the plane, shrinking and fogging with a smoke trail. Air targets stay fast.
- **Breakthrough drama:**
  - In Ringfall, 55% of units are near misses: the allies stop them within 22 texels of the fire line (`groundNearMiss`), and nothing fires in sectors 1–2.
  - Sectors 3–4 guarantee one telegraphed breakthrough per visit. If nothing has broken through 9 s in, the next arrival is unescorted and cannot be locked on to until it reaches fire range. It is announced by `allyUnderAttack`, then `groundBreakthroughWarning` 3 s ahead.

**Round 4: ground war v4 (`openspec/changes/ground-war-v4`): far more war, same threat.**
- **Skirmish layer:** beside the unchanged threat front, mixed demon forces push in from the horizon across the whole ground plane in arrival groups, with a 34 s surge-and-lull tide, and fight the convoy on the visible ground. Skirmish units hold short of fire range, never fire at the player, never move pressure or allied strength, and are not homing targets. A battered ally falls back to its escort slot and repairs; it is never totalled by a skirmish. The layer has its own random stream, so the threat front and the air war replay exactly. Proof (`test/ground_war_v4_sim_test.dart`): breakthroughs and ground shots at the player are unchanged in every sector (L1–L2 zero), and visible engagements per minute rise from 7.0/8.2/4.4/4.2 to 24.6/27.0/27.1/30.7 in L1–L4, plus 11.9 in the carrier sector, which had none.
- **Mixed forces by level:** Krybor grunts beside the sector vehicles; rocket troopers from Ringfall; demon heavy tanks from Ember. The convoy mixes vehicles with rifle and rocket squads. Demon troopers are gargoyle demons in military attire (helmet over the horns, vest and webbing, folded bat wings, barbed tail); the fallen crack into stone.
- **Full-width manoeuvre:** skirmish units steer laterally across the whole plane (threat units weave inside their side band, so missile launches and dodges are unchanged) along valley floors and around hills, spreads from its neighbours and flanks its foe. The convoy's escort slots follow the ship; engaged allies close on their foe, then fall back. This is positional only: the rules read depth.
- **Readability:** allies draw 1.3× larger with a cyan IFF chevron; a plant in front of a unit stays in front; small-arms, rocket and tank-shell tracers.

**Events** (`lib/game/game_events.dart`, one no-op-safe dispatcher drained in `main.dart`): `groundForceSighted`, `groundForceInRange`, `groundMissileLaunch`, `homingPickup`, `homingLaunch`, `homingHit`, `groundUnitDestroyed`, `allyUnderAttack`, `allySaved`, `allyRetreated` (alias `allyLost`), `fleetIntact`, `groundBreakthrough` and `groundPressure {level}`; `groundUnitDestroyed` carries `by: allies | homing`. Every event carries `sector` and, where positional, `x`/`y`. SFX: `missileLaunch`, `missileIncoming`, `homingLock` and `groundExplosion`, synthesised in `web/audio/demon-audio.js`. Full design and tunables: `openspec/changes/add-ground-forces-homing/design.md`, as retuned by `openspec/changes/tune-ground-war-front/design.md`.

## Resume and browser history

Save a small checkpoint at each wave/boss entrance, not on every frame. Fields include schema version, difficulty, route, sector, wave, score, lives, medal count, kills, heart slots and active milliseconds. Resume restarts that entrance without powerups or transient entities. It restores score and accumulated time from the same entrance, so unfinished-wave progress is replayed. New runs replace the checkpoint; game over and completion clear it. Returning to title preserves the latest entrance.

New saves use schema 2 and `heartParts: 4`, with bottom-left/bottom-right/top-left/top-right bits 1/2/4/8 (complete mask 15). A save without that marker maps legacy lower bits unchanged and old top bit 4 to both new top quarters (12).

Browser local storage holds checkpoints and separate unlock history, through a safe platform abstraction. Valid older snapshots receive defaults for absent optional fields; extra fields and schema-version differences do not by themselves reject a checkpoint. Invalid required route/wave data disables Resume without blocking a new game. History persists failures and unlock events independently so replaying a checkpoint cannot revoke an unlock. Clearing browser data removes this device-local history.

## Presentation and scores

HUD uses the named destination, score, lives, heart inventory and power status. Backgrounds retain the original world artwork, adding independently scrolling terrain, silhouettes and structures on deeper routes. Larger concise story captions and travel bars connect destinations, with each story phase extended by one third of a second. District silhouettes make slum roofs, dock cranes, mountain ridges and command pylons visible in separate parallax layers. Travel/reward captions connect destinations, carrier discoveries and fleet recognition; first-wave arrivals instead show a contextual non-repeating shuffled hint with five-to-nine words and one matching pixel illustration. The controller status overlays its panel without shifting the drag-guidance label. Spread uses cyan side cannon glyphs, Rapid a gold central bolt, Powerful a coral central heavy cannon, Large amber side explosive shells, and Shield a violet outer outline.

Initials entry uses three rotating A–Z/0–9 wheels, initially AAA, with touch and keyboard controls. A neutral Top10 heading has mode-only tabs for unlocked difficulties; the lone Normal tab is hidden. Every row displays five medal positions, active time and score; five-medal rows also display remaining lives. Unknown legacy time displays an em dash. Failed submissions retain their metadata for retry.

The same-origin `/api/scores` Worker API uses the existing `global-top10-v1` SQLite Durable Object. GET accepts `difficulty=easy|normal|hard` (default Normal); POST accepts initials, score and optional difficulty, medals, activeMilliseconds and remainingLives. Legacy initials/score requests remain valid. Additive guarded migrations retain older rows as Normal with zero medals and unknown time. Boards retain ten scores independently, ordered by score descending and insertion ID ascending. Bounds and completion metadata are validated before writes. Lives metadata is permitted only for five-medal scores. Local unlocks do not prevent direct API access, and client-reported scores are not cheat-proof.

## Validation and follow-up tuning

Verify all five routes end at exactly five medals, rewards happen once, and Hard cannot earn lives from hearts. Check density ceilings, rare-enemy overlap, symmetric shield/hatch geometry and closed-door core protection. Exercise complementary and duplicate heart storage, old and malformed checkpoints, unlock permanence, active-time exclusions, and independent board migration/pruning. Browser review should cover narrow phones and desktop, controller guidance, long captions, medals, power combinations, audio unlock, pause/resume and network retry. Deployment remains a separate action after local playtesting.

Offensive calibration preserves baseline 1 and Powerful 2 damage in every mode, including Large combinations. Large provides coverage rather than another damage multiplier: radius 32/30/28 in Easy/Normal/Hard (effective ordinary center reach 46/44/42). Route movement growth is 4% per tour step rather than 10%, and hostile mode rates are 0.9/1/1.05. Player movement, normal firing cadence, mutual exclusions and power duration remain unchanged.
