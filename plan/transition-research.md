# Level transitions: researched design and implemented music

Research accessed 2026-10-20. The references below are primary engine/audio documentation; the design timings are our recommendations, not claims that those engines prescribe specific durations.

## Principles and application

Audiokinetic separates music transitions, transition segments, and event-triggered stingers in its [interactive music documentation](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/public-library/2024.1.8_8893/?id=creating_interactive_music&source=Help). Its [stinger tutorial](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/learn/videos/flrz463kvei?course=wwise301&lesson=1) demonstrates triggering a short musical response from gameplay. Its [Music Segment properties](https://www.audiokinetic.com/library/2024.1.2_8726/?id=music_segment_property_editor&source=Help) describe beat/bar/grid synchronization from tempo and time signature. Applied here: distinguish immediate achievement feedback from the travel interlude, while retaining a shared 120 BPM clock for subsequent musical handoffs. Ordinary background changes can wait for a bar; the final kill should receive immediate acknowledgment.

Godot's [Tween documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_tween.html) describes interpolating properties, parallel/sequential steps, and easing. Applied here: one normalized cutscene clock should drive ship ascent, star streak length, planetary parallax, caption opacity, and destination arrival. Use ease-in acceleration followed by an ease-out approach, with overlapping fades; avoid popping the entire scene to a new background. This is an engine-independent animation approach and needs no Godot dependency in Flutter/Flame.

Recommended scene structure: celebrate a cleared sector for about four seconds while tallying earned points; hold the ship and stop hostile spawns. Then accelerate into a short travel passage, overlap outgoing and incoming celestial palettes, and decelerate into a readable arrival/wave announcement. Treat waves as reinforcements inside the same level, with a small ascending stinger instead of repeating the full clear ceremony. Reserve the larger carrier victory theme for the boss defeat.

## Implemented audio contract

The existing `window.demonAudio.cue(mode, level, phase, cycle)` API now accepts `levelclear` and `travel` alongside existing modes. New assets are original Standard MIDI files generated by `web/audio/generate-midi.py`:

- `levelclear.mid`: an eight-beat/four-second major victory phrase, distinct from the carrier finale. Begins as soon as its preloaded score is ready, resets the music grid, and fades previous scheduled music voices over 90 ms. Sound effects are preserved. Its final major cadence repeats while awaiting the next cue, so a longer score tally does not fall silent.
- `travel.mid`: a sixteen-beat/eight-second looping suspended-chord interlude with rising arpeggios. It enters on the next four-beat boundary and returns to the next level cue on the same grid. Release tails overlap these handoffs.
- `sfx('wave')`: a three-note ascending reinforcement signal, layered without replacing the active score.

Both new MIDI tracks preload at script initialization to keep level-clear feedback responsive. The boss `victory` cue, its sustaining cadence, and all four evolving boss music phases remain intact. No recorded or third-party audio is used.

The model/UI should emit `levelclear` after the last wave, `travel` when the journey starts, `level` on arrival, and `wave` at intra-level reinforcements. Keep the game clock and cutscene timings authoritative; music follows events, never changes gameplay state.

## Validation

`npm run test:worker` passes. The audio test loads the actual generated MIDI files and verifies immediate level-clear note scheduling, continuing cadence beyond the opening, audible travel scheduling, three wave-stinger notes, and return to the next level. Existing boss-phase, victory, mute, and pause/resume checks remain. `node --check web/audio/demon-audio.js` passes. Subjective balance and animation/music pacing still require browser listening/playtesting.
