# Proposal

## Why
The existing short music loops are conspicuous, especially across level transitions; comms sound degraded and mouth animation feels unnatural. Production now needs a cohesive campaign score and expressive, playful radio, with recoverable multi-day local rendering.

## What Changes
- Compose one original, evolving Battle Metal score across all five levels, waves, transitions and final boss; longer sections, recurring themes, synchronized adaptive layers, wordless vocals and event accents.
- Preserve the metal character of levels 1, 3 and 4; strengthen 2 and 5 while allowing a slow opening and a campaign-wide crescendo.
- Re-render music and radio from lossless masters, improve mouths, and retain character identities when economical.
- Give demons more airtime, original reverse/pitch-treated performances, consistent alien writing and slightly delayed translations; playful human/demon comeback exchanges.
- Use one full-width conversation toast for linked human/demon exchanges, with the first portrait on the left and the comeback portrait on the right, both texts and alien script/translation. Isolated messages each occupy half a toast, allowing two independent messages at once.
- Scatter unobtrusive clues throughout battle chatter; after completing all five levels, reveal teenage humans and demons playing a board game at a San Diego beach, sharing burgers, fries and sodas. Follow a brief cinematic with credits.
- Migrate orchestration to Lua and demanding processing to Rust or Go; retain Python for necessary model libraries. Keep resumable ledgers and frequent remote checkpoints.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
None.

### Modified Capabilities
- `arcade-presentation`: continuous adaptive recorded music, paired comms, expressive animation and beach ending with credits.

## Impact
Flutter comms/director/presentation, web audio and music director, music/comms source specifications, local rendering tools and manifests, ending artwork, and production documentation. Existing weapons and effect audio remain accepted unless a documented musical integration warrants replacement. No production deployment is implied.
