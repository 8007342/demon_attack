# End-game cinematic: style guide

Change `endgame-cinematic`. This guide fixes the look, the cast, the plastic figures, the colour script and the tone
of the ending. The shots are in `art/storyboards/endgame/README.md`, the details and their plants in
`plan/endgame-reveal-ledger.md`, the research in `plan/endgame-cinematic-research.md` (Part A craft, Part B
pipeline). Where a rule here rests on research, it names the section; the sources sit there. Everything else is a
design decision of this change, taken on 2026-11-16, and the operator can override it.

## 1. What the scene has to do

1. Show, in about 29 seconds, that the war was a board game played by kids on the patio of Krybor, a beachfront
   burger place.
2. Take nothing back. The kids won the battle the player won, and the scene celebrates it (research A3).
3. Make the player want a second run. Every shot is packed with things the game has already shown or said, and the
   scene explains none of them.
4. Look like a frame from a well-lit animated feature, held still. We do not try to match feature-film character
   animation; no local route reaches it (research B1). We spend the quality on design, light, lenses and sound.

## 2. Tone rules

- **Warm, never ironic.** Nobody is mocked: not the kids for pretending, not the player for believing.
- **Show the parts, not the sum.** No caption, narrator or line says "it was a game" (research A1). The lines are
  what people at a restaurant say: refills, an order, time to go home.
- **Each voice keeps its relationship to the player.** The Admiral looked after the pilot, and Mom does. The brood
  teased and gave ground, and the waitress teases and pours. The Marshal ran Krybor's sky, and the owner runs
  Krybor.
- **Family friendly.** Kids are kids: fully dressed for a beach restaurant, doing nothing but playing, eating and
  laughing. No danger, no romance, no jokes at anyone's body, accent or origin (`art/comms/cast.json`, comment).
- **Two layers of joke.** The surface is for young players (the grumpy general smiles, free soda). The second layer
  is for older ones: the table number, the menu, the cabinet in the corner, a rematch "after a short break". Nods
  are original phrasing. Nothing is quoted or copied from a film, a game or a brand.
- **One twist.** The room explains the game. The room is not itself revealed to be something else. The button shot
  S8 is a wink, not a second twist.
- **No real people, places or products.** Krybor is invented. The board game's box, the soda, the cabinet and the
  van are original designs with no real logo, trade dress or likeness.

## 3. The look

| Aspect | Rule |
|---|---|
| Rendering style | Stylised 3D: rounded forms, slightly large heads and hands, simple readable faces, soft skin, real-world materials (worn wood, scuffed plastic, condensation on glass). Prompts and briefs describe these traits. They never name a studio, a film or an artist |
| Lens | Long and shallow at the table (85 mm look in S1 and S8, 50 mm in S4 to S6), wider as the world opens (35 mm in S2 and S3, 24 mm in S7). The lens tells the size of the world: the toy is shot like a miniature, the patio like a place |
| Focus | Very shallow at board level in S1, deepening as the camera rises in S2. One rack focus, in S4 |
| Light | One low sun from the sea side, behind the table: warm rim on every head, soft bounce from the sand and the table top. Practical lights come on in S7: the neon sign and the patio lanterns |
| Time of day | Late golden hour moving to dusk across the 29 seconds |
| Grain and grade | One shared grade and one fine grain on every plate, applied in the packer, so stills from different runs sit together |
| Game frame in S1 | The game's own pixels, untouched, up to the cut |
| Motion | Limited on purpose: camera moves, parallax, a rack focus, light changes, and a few pose swaps (a blink, a head turn, arms up). Each pose swap is held like a drawn pose. No morphing between poses |

Master plates are 4:3. The landscape frame is the 16:9 band of the plate; the portrait frame is a 9:16 column
that the camera places per shot. Every shot keeps its subject inside the portrait-safe column drawn as white dashes
on the boards.

## 4. The cast as bystanders

The kids cast the people around them. Their own families became the fleet; the restaurant's staff became the
brood. The kids imagine every grown-up older and grander than they are, and themselves older too.

Each bystander must read in two seconds (research A8). So each has one silhouette, one accent colour (their radio
portrait's), one prop and one gesture, and shares the accent with their plastic figure and with nobody else in the
frame.

## 5. The board and its figures

- **One mould per figure.** Each figure is one colour of slightly glossy injection-moulded plastic, with a round
  base, a visible mould seam, a sprue mark under the base and soft edges where a kid's thumb has worn it. No paint
  except a sticker.
- **Colours are the game's.** Brood figures come in the five brood colours: teal, violet, coral, gold and crimson.
  Fleet ships are white with a cyan chevron sticker. Ground forces are olive. A figure's silhouette is the game
  sprite's silhouette at the board's eye level, so a player recognises each one.
- **The carrier** is the largest piece, violet, in two parts that click apart: its hatches are real little doors.
  The S1 toy is rendered from the game's own carrier mesh, so the match cut lines up (research B9).
- **Scale.** A gargoyle is 4 cm tall, a ship 5 cm, a tank 4 cm long, the carrier 18 cm. Hands in frame keep the
  scale honest.
- **The board** is a folding card board, worn at the fold, with five regions in the sector colours (Krybor blue,
  Ringfall violet, Ember Moon coral, the Hollow green, the Carrier red-black; `plan/sector-style-guide.md`) and
  a track of squares. One corner has sand on it. KRYBOR is printed where the carrier lands.
- **On and around it** (each is a ledger row): two dice, a dice cup, URGENT MISSION cards, five power cards, a sand
  timer, sugar packets in the power-up colours, heart tokens, a stamp card with four heart quarters, bottle caps,
  two gold tokens, a score pad, the kids' menu placemat with the brood alphabet, a soda ring, three fries in a row.
- **The box lid** reads DEMON ATTACK, with a corner flash for the edition (ledger R4). It is an original design.
- **Stillness.** Pieces never move unless a hand moves them. A figure that drifts between frames is a reject.

## 6. Colour script

The game is neon on near-black. The room is warm light on warm things. The script walks from one to the other and
then lets the neon come back once, as a sign (research A7).

- Saturation falls from S1 to S6 and rises once in S7. Value rises at the cut and falls gently to S8.
- Violet is in every shot: the carrier, the toy, a figure, her hair, a hood in shadow, the sign, a figure on the
  shelf. It is the thread from the game to the room.
- The boards use this palette; `art/storyboards/endgame/boards.lua` holds the hex values.

## 7. Staging and camera

- One line of action: the fleet side is frame left, the brood side frame right, the camera on the land side, the
  sea behind the table, the building frame right (storyboard README, "Geography"). S5 is the only insert off it.
- One idea per shot. A shot ends when its idea has landed.
- One showpiece move: the rise in S2. S7 rhymes with it. S3 and S5 drift by no more than 3 percent of the frame.
  No whip pan, no shake, no zoom.
- The eye is led across each cut: the subject of the next shot starts within one sixth of the frame width of where
  the last one ended (our rule; research A5 has the background).
- Adults enter from fixed sides: staff from the right, Mom from the left.
- Mouths: speakers are turned away, small in frame, or shown with two or three held mouth poses timed to the clip
  (research B8). No generated lip movement.

## 8. Text on screen

- **The sign** reads KRYBOR, in the game's title lettering style, with a teal chevron. It is its own layer, built
  from type, never generated, and is checked letter by letter.
- **Captions** stay as shipped: "SAN DIEGO · AFTER THE LAST TURN", then "Same friends. Next round?".
- **Subtitles** are one line each, on by default, with the role first and the radio name on the first use only:
  "MOM (the Admiral)". They are drawn by the game, so they can be translated.
- **Small print** in the set (menu, chalkboard, tickets, box lid, placemat) is real text on its own layers. The
  menu: Frontier Fries, Ringfall Rings, Ember Moon Burger, Hollow Tower, The Carrier (feeds five).

## 9. Sound

- The room arrives before the picture: ambience starts 0.6 s before the cut (research A9).
- The radio effect leaves the Admiral's voice across the cut and never returns.
- The battle track turns into a small speaker in the room and fades under the surf.
- Four game sounds return as room sounds: shield hum as soda fizz, pickup chime as ice and bell, forge roar as
  grill, carrier field hum as neon.
- Voices: the cast's own identities through a "room" chain with no band-pass, squelch or saturation
  (`openspec/changes/voice-stack`). Kids' lines need two child identities. Until clips exist, subtitles only.

## 10. Never

- Never explain a detail. No arrows, no flashbacks, no side-by-side comparison.
- Never show the pilot's face or give the pilot a voice.
- Never make the war "not count": no line belittles the game, and the score entry follows the credits as always.
- Never show a real brand, a real restaurant, a real arcade machine or a real board game.
- Never let a generated image decide a name, a number or a sign.
- Never flash more than three times in a second, and never cut to white.
- Never put a plant in the game that only makes sense after the ending. On a first run each one is plain battle
  chatter or plain scenery.
